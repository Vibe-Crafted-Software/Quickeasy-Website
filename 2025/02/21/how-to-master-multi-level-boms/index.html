How to Master Multi-Level BOMs

21 February 2025

How to Master Multi-Level BOMs: The Key to Efficient Pharma & Manufacturing Production. In industries like pharmaceuticals and manufacturing, precision is everything. Whether you're producing life-saving pills, complex chemical compounds, or industrial components, the ability to manage multi-level Bills of Materials (BOMs) is crucial. But without the right tools, it's easy for production planning to become a logistical nightmare.

First, What is a Multi-Level BOM?

A multi-level BOM is a hierarchical breakdown of materials, components, and sub-assemblies required to manufacture a finished product. Unlike a simple BOM, which only lists end-product ingredients or components, a multi-level BOM details intermediate stages, linking raw materials to sub-assemblies before reaching the final product.

For instance, in pharmaceutical manufacturing, a cough syrup may have:

  • A bulk syrup formulation (primary BOM)
  • Active ingredients, preservatives, and flavouring agents (sub-BOMs)
  • Final packaging and labelling (packaging BOM)

Each of these levels needs precise coordination to ensure traceability, compliance, and efficiency in production.

Why Multi-Level BOMs are a Headache

1. Complexity of Recipes and Formulations

Pharmaceutical and manufacturing industries deal with intricate formulations. In these industries even minor errors can compromise product integrity. A multi-level BOM ensures that each component is linked correctly, but keeping track manually is impractical. Scaling recipes based on production batch sizes further complicates matters.

2. Unit of Measure (UOM) Conversions

Manufacturers frequently convert materials between different UOMs. A drug's active ingredient might be measured in milligrams, while its bulk formulation is in litres, and final packaging in millilitres per bottle. Errors in UOM conversions can lead to wastage, miscalculations, or even regulatory non-compliance.

3. Batch and Lot Tracking

In industries governed by stringent quality regulations, such as pharma, each batch must be tracked from raw material to finished product. This ensures compliance with Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and allows for rapid recall if defects arise. A multi-level BOM must integrate seamlessly with batch tracking to maintain full traceability.

4. Version Control and Approvals

Manufacturers frequently refine formulas and processes. A new version of a drug formula, for instance, may require approval before production. Managing multiple BOM versions manually can lead to outdated specifications being used, causing costly rework and compliance risks.

5. Integration with Production Planning

Without seamless integration, production teams may not have the correct materials or components at the right time, leading to delays, bottlenecks, or excessive inventory holding costs. A disconnected BOM system can also cause disruptions in scheduling and production flow.

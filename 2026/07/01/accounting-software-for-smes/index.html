What is accounting software?

Accounting software is the system your business uses to record, process and report financial transactions.

At a basic level, accounting software helps you manage:

  • Income
  • Expenses
  • Customer invoices
  • Supplier invoices
  • Payments
  • VAT
  • Bank transactions
  • Debtors
  • Creditors
  • Trial balance
  • Income statement
  • Balance sheet
  • Budgets
  • Financial reports

For a small business, a standalone accounting package may be enough. If you sell a few services, issue a few invoices and do not carry stock or manage operational workflows, you may not need more than that.

But many SMEs are not that simple. You may have stock. You may buy from multiple suppliers. You may have sales reps quoting customers. You may manufacture, install, print, package, deliver, service or repair. You may need to track work in progress. You may need to see whether a job is actually profitable. You may have branches, entities, warehouses, projects, cost centres or customer credit rules. When those moving parts affect your numbers, your accounting software needs to be connected to them. That is where QuickEasy BOS ERP fits in.

Why spreadsheets and standalone accounting packages become a problem

Most bookkeepers do not use paper-based ledgers anymore. That has not been the norm for a long time.

In most SMEs, the finance process usually sits in one of two places: spreadsheets or standalone accounting packages.

Spreadsheets are flexible, but they are still manual.

Someone has to update them. Someone has to check the formulas. Someone has to make sure the right version is being used. Someone has to copy information from emails, job cards, supplier invoices, quotes, bank statements and stock reports. That can work for a while. Then the business gets busier.

QuickEasy reduces that risk by keeping the financial and operational parts of the business in one system.

What accounting functionality should SME accounting software include?

Good SME accounting software should do more than record invoices and expenses. It should help your business manage money, risk, compliance, reporting and operational cost.

QuickEasy includes the core accounting functionality you would expect from many well-known accounting packages, with the added benefit of ERP integration.

What QuickEasy Accounting Includes

GAAP and IFRS-compliant accounting

Your accounting system should support proper financial standards. QuickEasy’s accounting functionality supports GAAP and IFRS compliance. That gives SMEs a structured accounting environment that supports recognised reporting standards and financial control.

For business owners, this matters because your reports need to be trusted. For bookkeepers, it means the accounting function is built around proper financial logic, not only operational convenience.

General ledger and ledger views

Your general ledger is the foundation of your accounts. QuickEasy includes multiple ledger views, giving your finance team better visibility over transactions and financial detail. Instead of only seeing a flat report, you can work with different views for analysis, checking and transaction tracking. This is useful when your business needs to understand the detail behind the number. That level of visibility is critical when your business has more complexity than simple income and expenses.

Accounting error tracking

Accounting errors are easier to fix when they are found early. QuickEasy includes accounting error tracking, helping your team identify and resolve accounting errors in real time. That reduces the risk of mistakes carrying through to reports, VAT, month-end close or management decisions.

This is especially useful for SMEs where finance teams are busy and transactions move quickly. You do not want to find out at month-end that something went wrong weeks ago. You want the system to help flag issues while they can still be corrected properly.

Bank imports and bank reconciliation

Bank reconciliation should not take longer than it needs to. QuickEasy supports bank imports and bank reconciliation. This helps reduce manual entry, speeds up finance admin and lowers the risk of reconciliation errors.

For bookkeepers, this means less time typing bank lines into the system. It also means fewer mistakes caused by manual capture. For owners, it means your bank position can be reconciled more cleanly and more often. That gives you a better view of cash flow, receipts, supplier payments and outstanding items.

VAT reconciliation

VAT can become messy when transactions are late, incomplete or spread across different systems. QuickEasy includes VAT reconciliation functionality. It automates VAT calculations and supports multiple VAT models, helping your team manage VAT with more control. This is important because VAT touches many parts of the business.

  • Sales invoices affect VAT.
  • Supplier invoices affect VAT.
  • Credit notes affect VAT.
  • Payment timing can affect VAT.
  • Late entries can affect VAT.
  • Period close-offs can affect VAT.

When these transactions are scattered across spreadsheets, emails and separate systems, VAT becomes a checking exercise. When they are handled in QuickEasy, the VAT trail is easier to manage.

Debtors and accounts receivable

Cash flow depends on getting paid. That means your accounting software should help you do more than raise invoices. It should help you manage debtors properly.

QuickEasy supports debtors and accounts receivable management, including customer account visibility, customer deposits, allocation of receipts, customer statements, reminders and account control.

This is where integrated accounting becomes especially useful. If a customer is overdue, over limit or has payment issues, that information should not only sit with finance. Sales and customer-facing teams need visibility too.

QuickEasy helps reduce customer credit risk by linking customer information with accounting information.

So when a flagged customer wants to make another purchase, your team has better context before accepting the order. That protects the business. It also gives sales a fairer view of the customer relationship. A customer is not only a sales opportunity. They are also an account with payment behaviour, credit history and financial risk.

Creditors and accounts payable

Supplier costs need to be captured properly. If they are missed, delayed or linked to the wrong work, your numbers can become misleading.

QuickEasy supports creditors and supplier payment functionality, including supplier invoice processing, payment allocation, supplier discounts, supplier overpayments and supplier payment management. This helps bookkeepers manage supplier accounts more efficiently. It also helps owners see costs more accurately. That matters in any SME, but it is especially important for businesses where supplier costs affect job profitability, stock value, production cost or customer pricing.

Batch supplier payments

Paying suppliers one by one takes time and increases admin. QuickEasy supports batch processing for supplier payments. This helps finance teams process supplier payment runs more efficiently, especially at month-end or when the business handles a large number of supplier invoices.

For a bookkeeper, this means less repetitive payment admin. For the business, it means cleaner payment control and a better view of who has been paid, who still needs to be paid and what is due.

Multiple entities

Many SMEs grow into more than one entity, branch, division or trading structure. QuickEasy supports multiple entities, allowing separate financial reporting while keeping control centralised.

This is useful when you need to manage each entity properly, but also need group-level visibility. QuickEasy gives you that structure without forcing finance to maintain disconnected systems for each part of the business.

Multiple currencies

If your business imports, exports or deals with international suppliers, multiple currency support matters. QuickEasy supports foreign currencies with automatic conversions. That helps simplify accounting for international transactions and reduces manual currency work.

This is useful for SMEs that import stock, buy specialised materials, deal with overseas suppliers or quote customers in different currencies. It helps keep the financial record more consistent and reduces the risk of manual conversion mistakes.

Budgets and variance analysis

Accounting software should help you plan, not only report. QuickEasy includes accounting budgets and variance analysis. This allows your business to compare actual results against budgeted figures. For owners and managers, this helps turn accounting data into useful management information.

Contract billing

If your business bills customers on contracts, recurring work or service agreements, invoicing can become time-consuming. QuickEasy includes contract billing, which supports batch invoicing for contract-based billing. Instead of building every invoice manually, QuickEasy helps streamline the billing process.

Service billing

If you bill for time, work or service activity, your accounting software needs to connect timekeeping and invoicing. QuickEasy includes service billing, which integrates timekeeping with invoicing and supports customisable rates.

This helps ensure that billable work is captured and invoiced accurately. For service businesses, this reduces the risk of completed work not being invoiced. For bookkeepers, it means fewer manual checks between timesheets, job notes and customer invoices.

Project progress invoicing

Some work is not invoiced all at once. Project-based businesses may need to invoice in stages. That could be based on milestones, work completed, deposits, progress claims or agreed billing points.

QuickEasy supports project progress invoicing, helping you bill ongoing projects accurately and on time.

This is valuable because project invoicing can be difficult to manage in a basic accounting package. If the project sits in one system and the accounts sit in another, finance may not know what is ready to invoice. QuickEasy helps connect project progress to billing.

Landing costs

The true cost of stock is not always the supplier price. If you import stock or buy goods with freight, duties, handling, transport or other associated costs, those landing costs need to be allocated properly.

QuickEasy includes landing cost allocation, helping you calculate more accurate inventory values.

This matters because inaccurate stock costing affects:

  • Gross profit
  • Cost of sales
  • Selling prices
  • Inventory value
  • Margin reports
  • Management decisions

If landing costs are not allocated correctly, you may think you are making more margin than you really are.

QuickEasy helps give you a more accurate view of the cost behind the item.

Work in progress and inventory

For manufacturers, printers, packaging businesses, engineering companies, service businesses and project-based SMEs, work in progress matters. You need to know what is in stock, what has been issued, what is in production, what has been consumed and what still needs to be billed or completed.

QuickEasy includes work in progress and inventory functionality as part of its accounting and ERP environment. This helps connect operational activity to financial value. Instead of treating stock, production and accounting as separate admin areas, QuickEasy links them into one process. That gives you a clearer view of where money is sitting in the business.

Reporting and dashboards

Business owners need reports they can use. A report is not useful if the data behind it is incomplete, outdated or manually stitched together.

QuickEasy includes management reporting, accounting dashboards, interactive reporting and drill-down visibility across key business areas such as sales, inventory, accounting and cost of sales. The point is to give owners, managers and bookkeepers financial visibility they can trust.

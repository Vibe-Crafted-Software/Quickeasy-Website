Thai landing pages

The twelve Thai-market landing pages, with what each one targets and when to send a prospect to it. The English set is indexed separately at /landing/.

This page is deliberately unlisted. Nothing links to it, it is noindex,nofollow, it is excluded from sitemap.xml, and it carries no data-pagefind-body, so site search will never return it. Reach it by typing the URL. Please keep it that way — do not add it to the nav, the footer or any sweep that adds links.

Read this first

These are localised, not translated.

Every page follows the same PBSC structure as its English twin and makes the same product claims, but the problems, the numbers and the objections are the Thai ones.

  • The pain is local. Minimum wage at roughly ฿337–400 a day by province and a rising social-security ceiling, labour shortage on the floor, imported raw material with real lead times and landing costs, and OEM work where the customer charges for a late delivery.
  • The compliance answer is honest. BOS accounting is built to IFRS and GAAP, which is what TFRS is based on. VAT at 7%, withholding tax, Thai tax-invoice layouts and any link to the Revenue Department's e-Tax Invoice & e-Receipt system are raised on every page as a question for the demo, not claimed as a feature. Do not let a sales conversation promise more than that.
  • Grants and deductions are never claimed. The depa funds and the SME software deduction have their own registered-supplier conditions. The pages say to check the current position with the local team before anyone budgets around it.
  • The South Africa-only bits are flagged as such. The automatic paper-price feed on the print pages is a South African service; the Thai pages say so and point at supplier price lists instead. Do not quietly drop that sentence.
  • Who the buyer is contracting with — QuickEasy Software SA develops and owns the product, Vibe Crafted Software is the distribution and support partner in Thailand. Both names are on every page, because in this market the relationship is half the decision.
  • Still a machine-drafted first pass. Flag every one of these for native-speaker review before launch, the same as the rest of the /th/ tree.

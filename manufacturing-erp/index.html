Where the margin goes

Four places a manufacturing business loses money without seeing it.

None of these show up as a line on the income statement. They show up as a good year that somehow did not pay.

You quoted it in March and made it in July

The estimate came off a spreadsheet someone last updated when material prices were different. By the time the job ran, the price you agreed had expired — and nothing in the process told you.

You bought material you already owned

Stock on the system and stock on the floor are two different stories. Purchasing cannot see what production has already committed, so it gets ordered again and the cash sits on a shelf.

The delivery date was a guess

A whiteboard cannot tell you what a hot job pushes back. The promise gets made in the sales office and discovered on the floor, usually a week too late to do anything about it.

Everything gets typed in twice

Quote, job card, purchase order, delivery note, invoice — each one retyped from the last. Every hop is a chance to transcribe a quantity wrong, and someone downstream pays for it.

What changes

One system, and each of those four stops happening.

Not because the software is clever, but because there is only one copy of the job and everybody is working on it.

The cost model recalculates itself

A bill of materials plus a routing is the cost of the job: material, labour, machine time, overhead recovery and scrap allowance. Change a material rate once and every estimate that uses it is right again.

MRP nets demand against what you have

Material Requirements Planning looks at real job demand, subtracts free stock and what is already on order, and only then tells you what to buy. Replenishment levels are yours to set.

The planning board shows the knock-on

Real-time planning and scheduling dashboards make capacity visible before the promise is made, so you can see what expediting one job does to the six behind it.

The job is captured once

The quote becomes the job card, which drives the purchase orders, the delivery note and the invoice. Nothing is retyped, so nothing is transcribed wrong.

End to end

How a job moves through BOS.

Six steps, one record. The last one is the step most systems never get to.

01

Quote

The enquiry lands against the customer's full history. Products, prices, capacity, multi-currency and multi-quantity options are all in front of the estimator.

02

Bill of materials & routing

What it is made of and how it is made, priced from live rates. The product configurator handles the variants so you are not rebuilding a BOM for every option.

03

MRP & procurement

Demand is netted against stock and open orders. Purchase orders go out against the supplier you chose, with landing costs allocated automatically.

04

Schedule

The job takes a slot on a capacity-aware planning board, where the whole team — not just the planner — can see where it sits.

05

Shop floor

Manufacturing Data Collection books time and material as the work happens. Job tracking, alerts and NCR sign-off keep quality and cost visible in real time.

06

Invoice — and cost back

Delivery, invoice and ledger in the same system. Booked time and issued material post back against the estimate, so the next quote is better than this one.

Before you commit

The honest answers to what you are about to ask.

  • "ERP projects are a capital decision." Not this one. BOS is a month-to-month subscription with no lock-in and no initial capital outlay — the prices are published, per user, per month.
  • "We will be sold the cheap edition and then upsold." Every module listed above is in every subscription, along with all upgrades and helpdesk support. There is no edition that unlocks a function.
  • "We are not ready for the cloud." BOS runs on your own network or on a managed cloud server we host, patch and back up daily. You can start on one and move to the other.
  • "Our process is unusual." BOS can be configured to match any transaction flow, however simple or complicated. Configuration, custom reports and training are quoted and agreed before any work starts, so they never appear unannounced on an invoice.
  • "What happens to our data?" It stays yours. There is a backup utility in the subscription, and the managed cloud option backs the database up daily to a separate location.
  • "Who do we call?" A South African helpdesk, by email, telephone and remote access. QuickEasy has been building business systems here since 1998.

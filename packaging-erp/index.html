Packaging ERP

Specification, costing, scheduling, stock and accounting for packaging converters — with the substrate, tooling and waste arithmetic that decides whether a run was profitable.

Packaging is a margin business run at volume. The difference between a good job and a bad one is usually a few per cent of substrate, a die cost amortised over the wrong quantity, or a call-off that never got netted against the blanket order. QuickEasy BOS keeps all of that on one record.

Why generic ERP struggles here

A carton is not a widget.

Four things a packaging converter deals with daily that a general manufacturing system was never shaped for.

The product is a structure, not a part

Substrate, adhesive, ink, coating and lamination, each with its own dimension and its own cost, combining into a specification that has to be priced as a whole.

Tooling has to land on the right run

Dies, plates and cylinders are a real cost amortised over a quantity. Put it on the wrong quantity and you either lose the order or lose the margin.

Waste happens at every stage

Extrusion, lamination, printing, slitting, die-cutting — each stage takes its percentage. Costed as one blended figure at the end, it is never accurate for any particular job.

Orders arrive as call-offs

A blanket order for the year, released in drops against a minimum order quantity. Systems that assume one order equals one delivery cannot see what is left to run.

What BOS does about it

The specification is the cost model.

BOS Professional carries a fixed estimating module built for printing, packaging and signage, sitting on a full bill-of-materials and routing engine.

  • Multi-layer bills of materials. Substrate, adhesive, ink, coating and laminate held against the finished item, priced from live rates — change one and every specification using it reprices.
  • Waste costed per stage. Wastage is calculated as part of the estimate, with the exact cost of unavoidable waste carried into the quote rather than added as a round percentage.
  • Layout guidance. BOS shows you how to lay the job out to reduce wastage — on a long converting run, a better arrangement is money every single hour.
  • Tooling amortised across the quantity. Dies and plates priced into the run they belong to, with multi-quantity estimating so you can see the break points before you commit.
  • Multiple units of measure. Buy by the tonne or the roll, consume by the square metre or the thousand, and let the system do the conversion every time instead of an estimator doing it once.
  • Batch traceability. Trace a finished product back to its production batch, including raw materials — for the customer audit and for the recall you hope never happens.
  • Call-offs against a blanket order. Order management and MRP that know how much of the contract is still to run.
  • NCR and quality sign-off. Non-conformance reporting and transaction approvals built in, not bolted on.

Common questions

What converters ask before they switch.

We do folding carton and some flexibles. Does one system cover both?

Yes. The bill of materials describes whatever structure the product actually has, and the routing describes whatever stages it actually passes through, so corrugated, folding carton, flexible and labels are all configured rather than coded.

Does it handle print as well as converting?

It does — the same fixed estimating module covers printing, signage and packaging. Many QuickEasy customers run all three. See printing, signage & packaging software.

Can we keep our costing logic?

BOS can be configured to match any transaction flow, however simple or complicated. Machine speeds, waste factors and overhead recovery are set to your plant, not to an industry average.

What does it cost?

Month-to-month, per user, no capital outlay, no lock-in, every module included. The prices are published.

On our servers or yours?

Either. Your own network, or a managed cloud server we host, patch and back up daily to a separate location.

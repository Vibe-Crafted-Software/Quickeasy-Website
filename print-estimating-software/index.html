Why spreadsheets stop working

The estimate is where a print job makes or loses its money.

By the time the job is on the press, the margin has already been decided. These are the four ways a spreadsheet decides it badly.

The layout was never optimised

Nobody had time to work out whether the job fits 4-up or 6-up on the sheet you actually hold. The difference between those two answers is often the whole margin.

Waste is a round number

Make-ready sheets, run spoilage, offcuts and the reprint when colour drifts get covered by "add 10%". Sometimes that is generous, sometimes it is nowhere near enough, and you never find out which.

Every press is priced the same

One blended hourly rate means short digital runs subsidise long litho runs, or the other way round. Either way you are winning the work you should be losing.

It all lives with one estimator

The rates, the tricks and the judgement are in one person's spreadsheet and one person's head. When they are on leave, quoting slows down; when they leave, it stops.

What the module actually does

Don't estimate. Be precise.

BOS Professional's print estimating module is configured to your presses, your stocks and your overhead recovery — not to an industry average.

  • Imposition guidance. BOS shows you how to lay the job out to reduce wastage, so the saving is found before the job is committed.
  • Waste costed to the cent. Make-ready and run waste are calculated rather than estimated, and the exact cost of the waste you cannot avoid is carried into the quote.
  • Ink consumption in the cost. Coverage is priced as material instead of disappearing into an overhead rate.
  • Per-machine speeds and overheads. Each press, cutter, folder and finishing line runs at its own rate and recovers its own overhead.
  • Multi-quantity quoting. Price 500, 1 000 and 5 000 in a single estimate so the customer can see the break points.
  • Multi-currency. Quote in the currency the customer buys in, with the cost still landing in your books in rands.
  • Outwork and bought-in items. Trade finishing, plates and delivery priced into the job rather than remembered at invoicing.
  • Paper price updates included in the subscription for South African print companies, so the estimator is not working off last season's rates.
  • A junior can quote like a senior. The knowledge sits in the system, which is what makes the estimating desk safe to hand over.

Beyond estimating

What a full print MIS covers.

"Print MIS" started in the 1980s as estimating plus management information. A modern one has to carry the whole job — which is what makes BOS an ERP rather than a quoting tool.

01

Estimate

Priced from your presses, stocks and waste factors, in multiple quantities and currencies.

02

Order

The accepted quote becomes the job card. Specification, quantity, stock and due date travel with it — nothing is retyped.

Substrate and outwork ordered against the job, with multiple supplier prices per item and landing costs allocated automatically.

A planning board the whole shop can see, so the answer to "where is my job?" does not require a walk to the floor.

Invoice from the same record, then put the actual cost next to the estimate — the only way the next quote gets better.

Common questions

What printers ask before they switch.

Is this a quoting tool or a full system?

A full system. The estimating module is the part printers feel first, but it sits on the same database as stock, purchasing, production, CRM, invoicing and full-house accounting. That is what makes an estimate-versus-actual comparison possible at the end of the job.

Does it handle both litho and digital?

Yes. Each machine is configured with its own speed, make-ready, waste factors and overhead recovery, so a job quoted for the digital press is not priced as though it were going on litho. Wide-format and signage work is covered too — see signage software.

How much configuration does it need?

The module is a fixed one built for print, so you are configuring your machines, stocks and rates rather than building an estimating system from scratch. Configuration work is quoted and agreed before it starts — it is never added to an invoice unannounced.

What does it cost?

A month-to-month subscription, per user, with no capital outlay and no lock-in. Every module, every upgrade and the helpdesk are included. The prices are published.

Can we run it on our own server?

Yes — on your local network, or on a managed cloud server we host, patch and back up daily to a separate location. You can start on one and move to the other.

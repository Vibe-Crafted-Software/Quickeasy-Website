Why print is different

General business software was not built for this.

A system designed for repeat products assumes the thing you are selling has been made before. In print, signage and packaging it almost never has.

Every job is a one-off

Different size, different stock, different finishing, different run length. There is no history to price against, so the estimate is built from first principles — and usually under time pressure.

The substrate maths never lines up

You buy by the tonne, the ream or the roll and consume by the sheet or the square metre. Every conversion between those units is a place to lose a few per cent without anyone noticing.

Nobody costed the waste

Make-ready, run spoilage, offcuts, roll ends, the reprint when the colour drifts. It is real material and real machine time, and on most estimates it is a round number someone added at the end.

Half the day goes to "where's my job?"

Customers ring the person they know, who walks to the floor to find out. Multiply that by everyone in the building and it is a working day a week.

The difference

The estimator is the product.

BOS Professional carries a fixed estimating module built for printing, packaging and signage — not a generic quoting screen with print-sounding labels on it.

  • It prices the waste, not a guess at the waste. Wastage is calculated as part of the estimate and the exact cost of unavoidable waste is carried into the quote — to the cent, not to the nearest round-up.
  • It shows you how to lay the job out. BOS will show you an imposition that reduces wastage, so the saving is found before the job is committed rather than after the offcuts are in the bin.
  • Ink consumption is a cost, so it is costed. Coverage is part of the calculation instead of being absorbed into an overhead recovery rate that flatters short runs and punishes long ones.
  • Machine speed and shop-floor overheads are in the model. Each machine runs at its own rate and recovers its own overhead, so a job quoted for the digital press is not priced as though it were going on litho.
  • Multi-quantity and multi-currency, in one quote. Price 500, 1 000 and 5 000 in the same estimate, in the currency the customer buys in.
  • An inexperienced estimator quotes like an experienced one. The knowledge lives in the system, not in the one person who has been there twenty years — which is also what makes the desk safe to hand over.
  • Paper price updates are part of the subscription for South African print companies, so the rates the estimator is working from are not last season's.

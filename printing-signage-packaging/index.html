In print, the margin is decided at the estimate.

A fixed estimating module built for print, signage and packaging — waste, ink, machine speed and shop-floor overhead priced before you quote — and the stock, purchasing, scheduling and books that run behind it.

The problem

General business software was not built for this.

A system designed for repeat products assumes the thing you are selling has been made before. In print, signage and packaging it almost never has.

Every job is a one-off

Different size, stock, finishing and run length. No history to price against, so the estimate is built from first principles — usually under time pressure.

The substrate maths never lines up

You buy by the tonne, the ream or the roll and consume by the sheet or the square metre. Every conversion is a place to lose a few per cent unnoticed.

Nobody costed the waste

Make-ready, run spoilage, offcuts, roll ends, the reprint when colour drifts. Real material and real machine time, and on most estimates a round number added at the end.

Half the day goes to "where’s my job?"

Customers ring the person they know, who walks to the floor to find out. Multiply by everyone in the building and it is a working day a week.

The benefit

Quote it right the first time, whoever is on the desk.

Every job is bespoke, so there is no second chance to price it. Three things that change.

Waste priced, not guessed

The exact cost of unavoidable waste goes into the quote to the cent, and BOS shows you a layout that produces less of it.

A junior quotes like a senior

The machine speeds, waste factors and overhead recovery live in the system, not in the head of the one person who has been there twenty years.

Nobody walks to the floor

Work in progress is visible to everyone, so whoever answers the phone can answer the question.

The solution

The estimator is the product.

BOS Professional carries a fixed estimating module built for printing, packaging and signage — not a generic quoting screen with print-sounding labels on it.

  • It prices the waste, not a guess at it. Wastage is calculated as part of the estimate, and the exact cost of unavoidable waste is carried into the quote.
  • It shows you how to lay the job out. BOS suggests an imposition that reduces wastage, so the saving is found before the job is committed rather than after the offcuts are in the bin.
  • Ink consumption is a cost, so it is costed. Coverage is priced as material instead of being absorbed into an overhead rate that flatters short runs.
  • Machine speed and shop-floor overheads are in the model. Each press recovers its own overhead, so a digital job is not priced as though it were going on litho.
  • Multi-quantity and multi-currency in one quote. Price 500, 1 000 and 5 000 together, in the currency the customer buys in.
  • Paper price updates are included in the subscription for South African print companies, so the estimator is not working off last season’s rates.

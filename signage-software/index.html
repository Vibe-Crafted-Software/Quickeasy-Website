Signage Software for Sign Shops

Estimating, production, stock and invoicing for signage and wide-format businesses — including the install labour, travel and site work that press-shaped software has nowhere to put.

A sign job is not a print job with a bigger sheet. It is priced by the lineal or square metre, cut from a roll that leaves waste at both ends, mounted on a substrate, and then delivered by two people in a bakkie to a site that may or may not be ready. Where sign shops lose money

The job was profitable until the install.

Four costs that routinely miss the estimate, and all of them are real money.

Media waste off the roll

A 1 370 mm roll cut for a 900 mm panel wastes the balance whether you charge for it or not. Priced by the square metre of finished sign, that waste is invisible right up until the stock take.

Installation costed as an afterthought

Two installers, a vehicle, half a day, a return trip because the wall was not ready. It rarely appears in the quote as its own line, so it is rarely recovered.

Site surveys and rework

The measure-up, the permit, the second visit when the sign does not fit. Real hours, usually absorbed.

Every quote is rebuilt from scratch

Same substrates, same laminates, same frame profiles, quoted fresh each time from someone's memory of what they charged the last customer.

What BOS prices

Sign estimating that covers the whole job.

BOS Professional's fixed estimating module is built for printing, packaging and signage — configured to your printers, cutters, substrates and crews.

  • Lineal metre or square metre pricing. Quote the way the trade quotes, with the cost still calculated from what the roll actually costs you.
  • Media waste calculated, not guessed. Nesting on the roll, offcuts and roll ends are priced into the estimate rather than discovered at month end.
  • Ink consumption in the cost. Coverage is priced as material, which matters more on solid-colour wide format than almost anywhere else in print.
  • Substrate, laminate and frame as a bill of materials. Build the sign once and re-quote it in seconds when the customer changes the size.
  • Installation as a costed line. Crew hours, vehicle and travel priced into the job so the install is recovered instead of absorbed.
  • Multi-quantity and multi-currency. Price a one-off and a rollout of forty branches in the same estimate.
  • Machine speeds and overheads per device. The flatbed, the roll-to-roll and the router each carry their own rate.
  • Quote history that stays with the customer. Unlimited customer records, every interaction logged, so the repeat order is priced from the real job, not from memory.

Common questions

What sign shops ask before they switch.

We do signage and some print. Is that a problem?

It is the normal case. The same estimating module covers print, wide format and packaging, so a shop doing banners on Monday and business cards on Tuesday quotes both out of one system. See print estimating software.

Can it handle a multi-site rollout?

Yes. Quote it as a multi-quantity estimate, run it as one job with its own schedule, and track installation per site. Project management is included for the larger rollouts that do not fit a single job card.

Does it price installation properly?

Installation is costed like any other operation — crew hours at your rate, plus vehicle and travel — so it appears in the quote and is recovered in the invoice.

What does it cost?

Month-to-month, per user, no capital outlay, no lock-in. Every module, every upgrade and the helpdesk are included. The prices are published.

Is it only for big shops?

No. The subscription model works for a single-user shop as well as for a factory floor, and users can be added or removed as the team changes.
